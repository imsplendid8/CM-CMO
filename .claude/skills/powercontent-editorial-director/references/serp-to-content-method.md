# SERP To Content Method

## Inputs To Prefer

Use the strongest available sources in this order:

1. User-provided product, month, target audience, or attached material.
2. Repository data such as monthly SERP observations, related keyword history, autocomplete captures, product keyword sets, seasonal calendar, and past content history.
3. Public SERP or blog observations only when the user asks for current external context or when currentness materially matters.

Never imply that a SERP observation is live if it comes from archived or generated data. Label the basis. When a screenshot is the only input, first extract or request structured text fields before making strong claims about competitor patterns.

## Read The SERP As Signals

Separate SERP inputs into:

- Table stakes: messages most competitors already use and users expect to see.
- Saturated language: phrases that should not be repeated unless reworked.
- Whitespace: useful questions competitors under-explain.
- Intent clues: words suggesting price search, cancellation, claim, terms, one-day use, refund, or rider comparison.
- Seasonal triggers: event, weather, travel, school, holiday, or calendar-driven demand.
- Extension behavior: additional titles, additional descriptions, promotions, and sitelinks competitors rely on.
- Monthly movement: new brands, dropped brands, rising angles, and declining angles.

Do not copy competitor wording. Convert observations into a different editorial angle, then keep the same angle available for SA linkage.

## Monthly Variation

Annual events may repeat, but the material should not. For recurring seasons, vary at least one of:

- reader situation
- first question
- comparison axis
- rider or term focus
- thumbnail scene
- supporting keyword group
- CTA framing

Example for Chuseok:

- Year A: long-distance driving and driver insurance checklist.
- Year B: airport departure and travel insurance timing.
- Year C: vacant home, leakage, and home fire insurance checks.

Use the selected month and exact event dates when available. If Chuseok is not in the selected month, do not use Chuseok merely because it is near in the annual calendar.

## Cannibalization Check

Before recommending a direction, compare against past titles, target keywords, and body angles. Flag risk when:

- the main keyword is identical and the title asks the same question;
- the outline has the same section sequence;
- the same seasonal event and same product focus were used recently;
- the new content would compete with an existing stronger URL.

If risk is high, change the intent, angle, or keyword target. If the overlap is intentional, explain why.

## Keyword Expansion

For product terms or rider text, extract keyword candidates from:

- noun phrases;
- user situations;
- legal/administrative steps;
- claim timing questions;
- exclusions and limits;
- comparisons users are likely to search;
- autocomplete modifiers such as `1일`, `해지`, `청구`, `추가`, `환급`, `약관`.

Filter out TM, auto insurance, brand-home-only terms, and unrelated business lines unless explicitly requested.
